Natural State Medicinals Get the paperwork
Arkansas, 2016

People voted, and now there is a card

It is your name on a state registry, and you reach it through an account on the portal the Department of Health runs. You pull it up on your phone at the counter, or print it if you would rather hold paper. It does not come from a store, and it does not come from us.

With it, you can walk into a licensed dispensary and buy medicine. Without it, you cannot. That is the whole reason this page exists.

It takes about two weeks and costs fifty dollars, plus a doctor visit. When you are approved, the card lives in your account on the state portal, so you can carry it on your phone or print it. Ten short pages from here to the counter. Use the arrows at the bottom, or the arrow keys on your keyboard.

Page two, who it is for

First, is it you?

Three things have to be true before any of the rest matters. They are not hard, but the state does not bend on them.

1You live in Arkansas, and your license or state ID says so.
2You can get in to see an Arkansas medical doctor, an M.D. or a D.O.
3You are not in the military or the National Guard. Federal law says no, and nobody in Arkansas can wave that away.

A patient under eighteen can hold a card, but a parent or legal guardian has to sign on as the caregiver and do the buying.

Page four, step one of five

Start with what is in your wallet

The state wants proof you live here. Your Arkansas driver license or state ID is that proof, and the address on it has to be the address where you sleep.

Do this

Make a photocopy of the front of your ID, on a full sheet of paper. That copy goes in with everything else.

If you moved and the address is old, go fix that first, before anything else on this page. It is the most common reason applications come back. Stand in that line once now instead of twice later. And a card from another state will not work here, so if you live elsewhere, page nine is for you.

Page five, step two of five

Then a doctor signs one page

A doctor looks you over and signs a single page that names your condition. The state calls it a written certification. A video visit counts.

Do this

Print the patient application and carry the whole thing to the visit. The doctor's page is already inside it.

That signature goes stale in thirty days. If it reaches the state on day thirty one, the page is worth nothing and you pay for another visit. Upload it the same week you get it.

Page six, step three of five

Four things go up on the portal

You do this on the state's website, at mmj.adh.arkansas.gov. It is the way the state wants it, and it is faster. All four go together.

·The application, filled out.
·The page your doctor signed.
·The photocopy of your ID.
·Fifty dollars, which you do not get back, ever, for any reason.

If one thing is missing, it comes back. They do not hold it and wait for you to notice. Before you hit send, read your own name out loud against your ID, letter for letter, and do the same with your birthday.

You make an account, upload the pages, and pay the fifty dollars with a card. The portal also shows you where your application stands, which is something a mailed envelope will never do.

Page seven, step four of five

Now you wait, and then you check the portal

The state gets up to fourteen days. When they approve you, your card shows up in your account on the state portal, and you can use it from there.

Do this

Watch for the approval, then sign in to the portal. You can pull your card up on your phone, or print it and carry the paper.

Plenty of patients never carry anything but the version on their phone. A printed copy in the glove box is not a bad idea either, for the day the signal is poor.

Page eight, step five of five

Then you go, and you ask questions

Bring your card, on your phone or on paper, and bring your ID. The person behind the counter has done this a thousand times and would rather you ask than guess.

·You may buy two and a half ounces every fourteen days.
·The state counts it across every dispensary, so you cannot stretch it by driving to the next town.
·Ask what the register says your count is before you decide what to buy.

Ask for Natural State by name if you want ours. What a store keeps on the shelf changes week to week, and asking is how they learn what to order.

Page nine, the honest part

What it costs, and what it will not do

The state fee is fifty dollars a year. The doctor visit is separate, and most Arkansas clinics land between a hundred and fifty and two hundred fifty. Insurance does not cover either one yet, so plan for it the way you would a dental visit. Ask what a clinic charges when you call, since the price varies.

·The card lasts one year, does not renew itself, and nobody calls to remind you. Start again sixty days early, with a brand new page from a doctor.
·You cannot grow a plant at home. Not one, not in a closet. That is still a felony here.
·It does not cross a state line, even into a state that allows it. The road between belongs to the federal government.
·It does not cover driving impaired, and it does not cover anybody but you. Handing your medicine to a friend can cost you the card.
·Cannot get to a store? One person can be named your caregiver. They apply too, pass a background check, and pay thirty four dollars for it.
·Hold a card from another state? Arkansas will sell you a short visitor card if your condition is on the Arkansas list too.
The end of it

That is the whole road

Nobody memorizes this. Take the paperwork with you and let the page do the remembering.
